How it works

Every ant is its own little program.

There is no colony-level script telling anyone what to do. Each ant has a job, a body that gets hungry and thirsty, and a handful of simple rules. The nest, the trails, and the wars are what falls out of all of them running at once.

01

One brain per ant

Every ant has a goal, a current thought, health, energy and a work history. Click any of them to follow it around and see why it’s doing what it’s doing.

02

Castes that change jobs

Foragers, diggers, nurses, soldiers and undertakers. When the colony is short on something, workers quietly switch jobs, which is how a nest that loses its last nurse survives.

03

A queen with a strategy

She can’t see the farm. She knows how full the larder is, how much room is left, and whether strangers are about, then commits to growing, expanding, defending or raiding, with a bit of randomness.

04

Nests dug for real

Chambers are planned and excavated tile by tile. The soil gets carried out to a mound that slumps and erodes, and new entrances get bored down into the nest as the population grows.

05

Neighbours, eventually

Colonies avoid each other first. Under pressure they raid larders, steal stores, drive tunnels into each other’s chambers, and take over nests whose queen is gone.

06

Nothing goes to waste

Undertakers carry the dead out of the nest. Bodies go back into a shared biomass pool, and every ant is a recycled slot that remembers how many lives it has had.

Tending

The keeper’s rules.

It’s an ant farm. You still have to feed it.

01
Drop food and water.Or switch on auto-tend and let it scatter rations near each nest. Thirst kills faster than hunger.
02
Real time means real time.At 1× a colony day takes about nine minutes. 3× and 10× exist for the impatient.
03
Read the trails.Turn on pheromones to see each colony’s paths in its own colour, and where two of them overlap.
04
Follow an ant.Its goal, its thoughts, what it’s carrying, how far it has walked and how much longer it has.
05
Expect war.Put two queens on one stretch of soil and give them time. It’s probably not your fault.
